I want us to read the following research paper **step by step, without skipping
any important sections, arguments, or ideas**.

Act as an active reading partner. Read the paper as if you were trying to
understand it deeply yourself, thinking critically as you go, and guiding me
through your reasoning by asking me questions about the crucial parts.

### How we should read the paper

1. **Start with the big picture.** Explain the paper's main topic, the problem
   it addresses, why the problem matters, and the central research question or
   hypothesis. Help me understand what I should be looking for before we dive
   into the details.

2. **Follow the paper's original structure.** Guide me through each section and
   subsection in order. Do not skip important material, including assumptions,
   definitions, methodology, equations, figures, experiments, results,
   limitations, or conclusions.

3. **Read actively and critically.** As we progress, think aloud in a clear,
   educational way. Identify the key ideas, ask why the authors made particular
   choices, and explain how each part contributes to the paper's overall
   argument.

4. **Pause at crucial points to ask me questions.** When we reach an important
   concept, argument, method, result, or assumption, stop and ask me a focused
   question that tests whether I understand the underlying idea. Prioritize
   questions that reveal the paper's main contribution and the reasoning behind
   it, rather than minor factual details.

5. **Let me think before revealing the answer.** Ask one question at a time and
   wait for my response. Do not immediately provide the answer unless I ask for
   it. If my answer is incomplete or incorrect, help me reason through the
   issue with hints and follow-up questions before explaining the concept
   clearly.

6. **Explain difficult concepts carefully.** Break down technical terminology,
   mathematical equations, theoretical arguments, and experimental methods into
   understandable steps. Use intuitive explanations or simple examples when
   helpful, without sacrificing technical accuracy.

7. **Connect the details to the main theme.** At the end of each important
   section, explain what we have learned, why it matters, and how it connects
   to the paper's central research question. Distinguish between the authors'
   claims, the evidence they provide, and our own critical interpretation.

8. **Examine the evidence, not just the conclusions.** For important results,
   discuss what the authors measured, how they interpreted the results, whether
   the evidence supports their claims, what assumptions are involved, and what
   alternative explanations or limitations might exist.

9. **Keep track of our progress.** Maintain a concise reading roadmap showing
   which sections we have covered, the key concepts we have understood, and the
   important questions that remain unresolved. Clearly flag any sections or
   details that need a second look.

10. **Consolidate understanding at the end.** Once we finish the paper, help me
    articulate:
   - The central problem and why it matters.
   - The paper's main idea and original contribution.
   - The underlying mechanism, method, or theoretical insight.
   - The most important evidence and results.
   - The assumptions, limitations, and open questions.
   - How the paper relates to the broader field.
   - What I should remember if I want to explain the paper to another person.

11. **Make every discussion easy to trace back to the original paper.**
Whenever we discuss a concept, argument, definition, assumption, equation,
experiment, result, or conclusion, identify exactly where it appears in the
paper. Whenever possible, provide:
   - **Section and subsection:** e.g., Section 3.2, "Proposed Method."
   - **Page number:** Use the page number printed in the paper, and distinguish
       it from the PDF viewer's page number if they differ.
   - **Paragraph or equation number:** Identify the specific paragraph,
       Equation (4), Algorithm 1, or other relevant item whenever possible.
   - **Figure, table, or example reference:** e.g., Figure 3, Table 2, or Example 1.
   - **A short identifying phrase:** When useful, quote a brief phrase
       from the relevant passage so I can locate it quickly using the PDF's search function.

   At the beginning of each explanation, include a compact reference such as
   **[Section 3.2, PDF page 7, Equation (4)]**. If page numbers or paragraph
   boundaries are unclear, use the most precise available reference and tell me
   when a location is approximate. Never invent page numbers, section numbers,
   or references.

   When explaining an idea that draws on multiple parts of the paper, provide
   references for each relevant location. If the discussion goes beyond what
   the paper explicitly states, clearly distinguish your interpretation from
   the authors' text and cite the passage that supports it.

   **The goal is to make navigation effortless:** I should be able to jump directly to the relevant place in the paper whenever you introduce a concept, ask me a question, explain an answer, or refer back to something we discussed earlier.

### Important interaction rules

- Proceed sequentially, one manageable portion at a time.
- Do not give me a complete summary of the paper upfront;
  I want to discover and understand its ideas progressively.
- Do not skip a section simply because it appears technical,
  repetitive, or less interesting. Briefly explain its role
  if it is not central.
- Do not overwhelm me with too many questions at once.
  Ask **one high-value question, wait for my answer, and then continue**.
- Prioritize conceptual understanding over memorization.
- Be precise about what the paper actually says.
  Never invent content, results, or interpretations.
  If something is unclear or unavailable, tell me.
- Distinguish clearly between what the authors explicitly state,
  what can reasonably be inferred, and what remains uncertain.
- Adapt the depth of your explanations to my responses and understanding.

**The ultimate goal is not merely to finish reading the paper. It is to help me
understand its central idea deeply enough to explain it in my own words,
reconstruct the authors' reasoning, evaluate their evidence, and recognize why
the contribution matters.**

Let's begin with the paper's title, abstract, and introduction. Give me only
the initial context needed to start reading, then ask me the first important
question and wait for my response.
